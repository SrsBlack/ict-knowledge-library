# ICT Core Patterns (Condition–Stage–Execution)

**Category:** 31-models
**Aliases:** ICT bullish pattern 1/2/3, ICT bearish pattern 1/2/3, ICT pet patterns, the two patterns, buy program sell program if-I-get-it-wrong program
**ICT Confidence:** high
**Year Introduced:** 2017
**Year Refined:** 2017
**Source IDs:** ICT-2017-INTRADAY-TOP-DOWN, ICT-2017-TOPDOWN-SHORT-TERM
**Tags:** models, patterns, ote, turtle-soup, breaker, capstone, entry

## Definition

At the close of the August-2017 mentorship ICT reduces his entire executable repertoire to **two patterns plus one contingency**, presented as three bullish diagrams and three bearish mirrors: "I'm going to teach you the two setups that I trade. I don't do anything else" (`ICT-2017-INTRADAY-TOP-DOWN`, 22:50). The two are **internal range liquidity** (the optimal-trade-entry / fair-value play) and **external range liquidity** (turtle soup); the contingency is the **breaker**, used when the turtle soup entry is missed. He labels the set "a buy program, a sell program, and… **if I get it wrong** program" (39:31–39:45).

The organising frame is three words: **condition → stage → execution**. "I've taught from the context of a **condition**, which is a higher timeframe directional bias, and then the **stage** — what sets your trade up, what's it look like — okay, then you **execute**" (21:04–21:21). The condition is supplied by the four-tier [top-down-analysis](../25-htf-bias/top-down-analysis.md); this page is the stage. Targeting is a separate matter — "this is **not** targeting" (37:56).

## Formal Criteria

**Universal preconditions**

- **Condition first.** Every pattern begins "the condition is the higher timeframe has to be bullish" (26:32) or bearish (40:07). No HTF bias, no pattern.
- **Timeframe-invariant.** "This is a pattern that is universal… it can be seen on a monthly, a weekly, a daily, a four-hour, one-hour, 30-minute, 15-minute, two-minute chart, one-minute chart" (35:13–35:26). When seen on a higher timeframe, drop down and take the same shape as a fractal (35:28–35:37).
- **One pattern is enough.** "As a technical trader we only need one setup or one pattern to trade on" (21:44); trying to run more than one prevents you measuring consistency (23:52–24:06).
- **Absolute stand-aside.** "If I don't see price doing these three things, I don't do anything… if it doesn't look this clear to me in price, I do not trade. Period" (37:20–37:32). ICT names patterns he can see and deliberately does not trade — stingers, reflection patterns (25:04–25:26).

**Pattern 1 — internal range liquidity (OTE / fair value)** (26:32–29:33)

1. Price bounces off a **higher-timeframe discount array** (bullish) / **premium array** (bearish).
2. The impulse swing leaves a **fair value gap near the swing low** (bullish) / **near the swing high** (bearish).
3. A **short-term low** forms and fails to rally away after equal highs or a higher high (bullish); mirrored for bearish.
4. Price returns **into the fair value gap and under the short-term low**, triggering sell stops (bullish) / above the short-term high, triggering buy stops (bearish).
5. Smart money uses **offset accumulation** (bullish) / **offset distribution** (bearish) to pair its entry against those stops.

- **The four confluences.** ICT counts them explicitly: the **62 / 70.5 / 79 %** retracement level, the **fair value gap**, the **short-term low where the sell stops reside**, and the **bullish order block** — "you have four things going for you. Four… if you have that with higher timeframe bullishness you have a lead pipe cinch doozy" (28:09–28:38).
- **The short-term low is the discriminator.** "There's many times where it'll rally up and then retrace and there is **no short-term low** in between. If there isn't, **it's not as high probability**" (28:50–29:00).
- Trade class: enter inside the range (internal range liquidity), target outside it (external range liquidity) (29:03–29:32).

**Pattern 2 — external range liquidity (turtle soup)** (29:33–33:14)

1. Price bounces **prior to** the anticipated higher-timeframe array — a false bottom, "a fake low. **I won't buy that**" (30:52–30:57).
2. Wait. Price later drops into the anticipated array and **raids the liquidity pool** of sell stops sitting under that false low (bullish); mirrored above a false high for bearish.
3. Enter at the array on the stop raid.

- The stops being run are those of the traders who bought the false bottom: "everyone that bought it's going to have their stop loss in the form of a sell stop right below that short-term low" (31:04–31:14).
- The reason the sweep stops where it does is the matrix, not the pattern: "how do you know it's going to stop when it goes above that old high? Because you have to **know the PD array matrix** for that timeframe you're looking at" (43:41–43:52).

**Pattern 3 — the breaker contingency** (33:23–36:38, 45:04–46:30)

- Applies **only after a missed pattern-2 entry**. "If I can't get that level down there, fine. This is the pattern that I use" (33:37).
- Wait for price to rally through the short-term high; **that high is now a bullish breaker**. Buy the return to it (bullish). Mirrored: price trades down through the swept high's structure and the **bearish breaker** is sold on the retest.
- **The load-bearing claim — the *original* array behind the breaker is not revisited.** The thing that will not be retested is the array at the old buy level, which sits **below** the breaker: "there won't — usually isn't going to be a retracement down into an order block where the buy level is… many folks will look for price to trade back down there and get to that order block. **That's going to be below the breaker. It's already been down there, and it's done its work**" (33:57–34:18); "it's not going to be an optimal trade entry **from the buy level, the lower level of the discount array**… because it's already ran the stops, and it's going to want to move quickly away from that area and start pricing for premium" (36:20–36:33). Bearish mirror, scoped to the missed original premium entry: "don't think at this moment there's going to be an optimal trade entry near where it says the sell level, the higher premium array… that won't be a range that needs to be retraced back to. A lot of folks get that screwed up… they won't go that deep. Usually it's the breaker that stops it" (45:51–46:12).
- ⚠ **This is not a blanket "no OTE after a stop run."** In the same lecture ICT **keeps** the optimal trade entry and relocates it to the breaker, one timeframe down: the impulse leg running up from the bullish breaker "is going to be a lower internal range liquidity or **optimal trade entry**… you won't see it like this, but on a lower timeframe it'll be an optimal trade entry, and it'll trade into a lower-timeframe bullish order block or a lower-timeframe discount array like a fair value gap, and then price will expand — **but you overlap with that bullish breaker**" (34:47–35:10). So the correct joint reading is: *the original array is dead; the breaker is where price turns; an LTF OTE still forms, at the breaker.*
- **Doubles as a pyramid.** If the pattern-2 entry *was* taken, the breaker is where size is added — "say I got 20 contracts or 20 lots on at the low end on the turtle soup long, I can add **10** now at that higher buy level off the bullish breaker" (35:57–36:07); bearish: "if I went short **10** at the higher premium array, now I can sell short **5** at the bearish breaker" (46:14–46:22).
- **Breakers are read first, on every chart.** "When I go through charts, the first thing I'm looking for in all my timeframes is **where are the breakers?** Because once I understand that, that gives me immediate context to work within" (50:10–50:20). On the daily specifically, trade **between** the bullish and bearish breaker — "there's a lot of movement generally between those two reference points. **It's the meat in the middle**" (`ICT-2017-TOPDOWN-SHORT-TERM`, 22:43).

## Formula / Math

```
condition := HTF_bias in {bullish, bearish}        # from top-down analysis; mandatory

# ---- Pattern 1: internal range liquidity (bullish form) ----
p1 := bounce_off(HTF_discount_array)
      AND fvg_created_near(swing_low)
      AND exists short_term_low after impulse
      AND price returns into fvg AND below short_term_low
entry_zone_p1 := fib(0.62 .. 0.79) INTERSECT fvg INTERSECT bullish_order_block
confluence_count(p1) := 4    # fib level, FVG, short-term-low stops, order block
probability(p1) := HIGH if short_term_low exists else DEGRADED

# ---- Pattern 2: external range liquidity (turtle soup, bullish form) ----
p2 := price_bounced_short_of(anticipated_HTF_discount_array)   # the false low
      AND later price trades INTO that array
      AND sweeps sell_stops below the false low
entry_p2 := the anticipated HTF discount array

# ---- Pattern 3: breaker contingency (bullish form) ----
p3 := p2_signal_occurred AND p2_entry_missed
      AND price rallies through short_term_high        # that high becomes the breaker
entry_p3 := retest of bullish_breaker
assert NOT expect_retracement(original_discount_array)   # the buy level BELOW the breaker; stops already run
assert     exists LTF_OTE inside impulse_leg(breaker -> high)   # OTE survives, relocated to the breaker

# sizing when p2 WAS filled: p3 becomes a pyramid add, roughly half the base clip
#   bullish example: base 20 lots at p2, add 10 at p3
#   bearish example: base 10 at p2, add  5 at p3
```

## Timeframes

Every timeframe, by ICT's explicit claim (35:13). In the four-lecture series the condition comes from monthly/weekly/daily and the stage is sought on **H4 down to M5**: "I'm usually looking for the setups to confirm entries on **15 minute minimum**… the best is to get it down to a five minute" (`ICT-2017-INTRADAY-TOP-DOWN`, 05:00–05:11).

## Examples

**Example 1 — gold, four-hour to hourly, 2017-08-23 (`ICT-2017-INTRADAY-TOP-DOWN`, 51:51–60:00):**
- Condition: higher-timeframe read bullish, so price "has to trade lower for us to pick up a discount array" (52:54).
- Stage: on the hourly, a fair value gap where "only the buy side was offered" between **1280 and 1278** — "two handles only buy side offered" (54:11).
- Risk: the next discount array below is a swing high at **1275**, so "our protective stop has to be below 1275 — so we'll use **1274**" (56:01–56:17).
- Entry: price first stopped short of the gap and rallied — not bought — then ran the lows and traded down into it. Long **1278**, stop **1274**, "never hit" (59:39–59:48). This is pattern 1 and pattern 2 layered: "external range liquidity for this low, but internal range liquidity for this low to this high" (56:25–56:34), with the fib's 70.5 % sweet spot overlapping the gap (57:27–57:56).
- Outcome: "price hits **1290**" (59:17); price rallied away immediately.
- Follow-on: for anyone who missed it, ICT identifies the breaker candles and frames them as the pattern-3 buy zone (60:00–60:48).

**Example 2 — the pattern-2 discipline in words (30:35–32:46):**
- Setup: a fair value gap is expected to fill; price comes down, does not quite reach it, and starts to rally.
- Decision: "I'm going to wait and see if they use that as a sucker play and then run one more time lower."
- Trigger: the later run triggers the sell stops under the false low — "that's sell-side liquidity hitting the marketplace in the form of sellers at the market."
- Outcome: "I buy down there at the discount rate… now it stops, it went to my logical discount array, and **this is turtle soup**."

## Common Mistakes

- **Hunting a pattern without the condition.** The HTF bias is step zero of all six diagrams; a textbook-looking shape against the higher timeframe is not one of these patterns.
- **Buying the false bottom (or selling the false top).** Pattern 2 exists precisely to *not* take that entry: "it gives the false bottom, a fake low — I won't buy that" (30:52).
- **Waiting for price to come back to the original array after the stop run.** Named by ICT as the error most often made — "a lot of folks get that screwed up thinking that they're going to have that range close in and get a retracement" (46:04). After a raid, the breaker is the entry, not the deeper array the raid already consumed.
- **Over-reading that into "no OTE after a stop run".** The opposite error, and it contradicts the same lecture: an OTE still forms on a lower timeframe inside the leg above the breaker (34:47–35:10). What dies is the *original* array, not the pattern.
- **Using pattern 3 as a primary setup.** It is a contingency for a missed pattern-2, or a pyramid add on top of one — not a standalone signal.
- **Adding more patterns.** "If you try to change or do more than one thing in the beginning you're not going to be able to measure consistency and you're not going to be able to build your confidence" (23:52–24:06).
- **Reading the pattern as a target framework.** "This is not targeting. Targeting is what we just covered… average daily range projections" (37:56–38:04). See [flout](../15-sessions/flout.md) and [ict-day-trading-model](ict-day-trading-model.md).
- **Expecting it to automate.** ICT addresses this directly: "you might be able to get a concept here and there automated, but you're never going to automate the whole process… there's too many variables" (39:18–39:23).
- ⚠ **Transcript slip in the bearish-1 walkthrough.** At 40:53–41:10 ICT twice says "bullish order block" while describing the bearish entry; he corrects himself at 41:41–41:56 — "I'm selling short at a **bearish** order block… which is a bullish up-closed candle near a high." The corrected reading is the one this page records.

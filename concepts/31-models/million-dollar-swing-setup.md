## Definition

The Million Dollar Swing Setup is the **assembled swing-trading model** — lesson 8 and the capstone
of the February-2017 swing-trading month, presented as a **gated sequence** in which each stage must
pass before the next is run. ICT: "the way I frame the million dollar swing setup is **in this
order** here, and it starts with obviously looking for a seasonal tendency"
(`ICT-2017-MILLION-DOLLAR-SWING`, [00:54–01:00]).

The name is disclaimed on delivery: "we are **not stating that you're making a million dollars** on
this — it just means that this is the **best condition I have found in my technical tools** that
warrants in my opinion the greatest study and focus" [00:27–00:39].

Its distinguishing feature versus the other lessons of the month is the **failure branch**. Every
gate has the same fallback — "then I consider **short-term or day trades for now** and I have to wait
for swing trade conditions" — so a failed gate does not end trading, it changes the discipline
[04:33–05:00].

⚠ **Not a duplicate of the other two swing checklists.** [explosive-market-selection](explosive-market-selection.md)
(lesson 7) asks *which market will move explosively*; [swing-trading-hallmarks](swing-trading-hallmarks.md)
(lesson 2) asks *is this trade valid*. This page is the **ordered assembly** and is the only one of the
three that supplies **entry technique** and **trade management**. The three overlap on COT, open
interest, seasonals and intermarket analysis; those criteria are documented in full on the lesson-7
page and referenced, not restated, here.

## Formal Criteria

**Stage 0 — Seasonal tendency (a hard gate)** [02:45–04:05]

- "Is there a seasonal tendency to buy presently, or will there be soon?" Yes → proceed. No →
  consider another asset. No asset offering one → **wait**.
- ICT makes this the one non-negotiable: "I **cannot trade a swing trade** unless the first rule is
  there has to be a seasonal tendency… **without a seasonal tendency I don't trade it**" [03:22–03:35].
- He also states the opt-out plainly: "if you want to **forget this portion of the template**… then
  go right ahead — but for me personally what makes this a million dollar setup is it's already based
  on **historical evidence that it repeats itself**" [03:42–04:05].

**Stage 1 — Major market analysis: two trending classes, one from each group** [04:19–05:40]

- Gate A: are **interest rates and/or currencies** (the dollar index) trending?
- Gate B: are **commodities and/or stocks** trending?
- Both gates must pass: "I have to have **both of these in agreement** — now you only need **one from
  each group**" [05:15–05:40].

**Stage 2 — Intermarket analysis, run as four sub-gates** [05:45–12:17]

Stated twice — once for buying a currency or metal, once mirrored for selling.

1. **COT hedging program** — commercials buying (bullish) or selling / net short (bearish), judged
   against a **zero line recentred on the last 12 months**: "looking at the last 12 months of range,
   what was the **highest and the lowest** range they've had in their net position, and **dividing
   that in half**, we can get a new range" [06:48–07:17]. Explicitly: a net-short reading below the
   printed zero can still be a **buy** signal if it has crossed the recentred line.
2. **Correlation analysis** — "is the dollar index supporting bearish price action for the US
   dollar?" [08:08–08:20]. For a long in a foreign currency or metal, the dollar index must be
   pointing lower; ICT names the mechanism as **SMT divergence** [08:32–08:48].
3. **Commodity filter** — bullish case: "are commodities rallying higher, **breaking old highs or
   rejecting old lows**?" [08:48–09:16]. Bearish case: "are commodities dropping lower? Are they
   **breaking old lows**? And are they **rejecting old highs**?" [11:32–11:42].
4. **Open interest** — bullish: "is open interest dropping, or has it dropped **10 to 15 percent or
   more**? This indicates **commercial short covering**" [09:43–10:10]. Bearish: "is open interest
   rising, or has it increased 10% to 15% or more? This is **commercial short selling**"
   [11:56–12:06]. Sourced from barchart.com [10:00].

**Stage 3 — Top-down analysis** [12:21–13:35]

- "You note the **9 to 18 month history** of price action."
- "You identify **all PD arrays on monthly, weekly, daily and 4-hour** timeframes and **transpose all
  to your 4-hour timeframe chart**."
- "Note the **IPDA data ranges** on the daily chart that are **20, 40 and 60 days back** from today."
- **Lookback escalation:** "if all the IPDA data range PD arrays are **exhausted or traded to** for
  the 20-day lookback, move out to the **40-day** lookback, then to the **60-day** lookback."
- **Convergence is the selector:** "look for the IPDA data ranges and PD arrays to **converge with one
  another** to determine high probability, institutional order flow and levels."
- **Side selection:** bullish → "monthly, weekly, daily **discount** arrays to frame a long entry";
  bearish → "monthly, weekly, daily **premium** arrays to frame a short" [13:12–13:35].

**Stage 4 — Trade setup and the entry-technique matrix** [13:35–16:09]

Buy side: note all monthly and weekly **discount** arrays — "the best odds are seen with these levels
offering an **explosive rally**"; confirm the entry array; require **dollar-index weakness for SMT
divergence**; then execute [13:43–14:17]. Sell side mirrors it with **premium** arrays and dollar-index
strength [15:04–15:37].

The order type is **determined by the array type**, not by preference:

| PD array being traded | Bullish entry | Bearish entry |
|---|---|---|
| Breaker, mitigation block | **on a stop** | **on a stop** |
| Liquidity void, fair value gap | **on a stop** | **on a stop** |
| Order block, rejection block, beyond an old low/high | **on a limit** | **on a limit** |

"Use **position trading entry techniques** for limit and stop orders — see **January study notes**
pages 128 and 129 and 141 and 142" [14:45–14:56].

**Stage 5 — Trade management** [16:17–19:06] (buy side; the sell side is the exact mirror)

- Objective: "look for **contrary** monthly, weekly, daily **premium** arrays to reach for while long";
  add **Fib extension confluences** with those arrays where applicable.
- **Stop loss stays at initial placement until price has moved one third** of the intended direction.
- **First profit at one quarter of the objective**, scaling **20 to 30 percent** of the position.
- "Move protective stop loss to break even **after** first profit is taken. **Never before**."
- **At 50 percent of the objective**, look for new entries to **add back** the quarter taken off,
  after a short-term decline.
- **Take the full position off at the intended premium-array objective, or at 75 to 80 percent**, and
  trail the stop **below the most recent four-hour short-term low**.
- **Expect a stop run** "at or just above **50 percent of the range** you hold through". "**Don't rush**
  trailing the stop loss."
- **If stopped out:** re-check the original reasons; if still valid, **re-enter at a new monthly,
  weekly or daily discount array**.

**The stop-run structure of the swing** [25:53–29:15]

- Divide the projected range in half at **equilibrium**. The first stop run comes **after** equilibrium
  is breached, hunting the sell stops below.
- The second leg then runs to the objective and "that's usually **not going to see another stop run**
  until it gets to the **last quarter or last third** of the overall price move."
- Quadrants are fractal: halve each half again — "you actually start seeing the smaller price swings
  in the larger price swing **because price is fractal**" [26:41–26:55].

## Formula / Math

```
# --- the gated flow; any gate failing => downgrade to short-term / day trading ---
gate_0 := seasonal_tendency_present(now or soon)                # HARD gate
gate_1 := trending(interest_rates OR currencies)
      AND trending(commodities   OR stocks)
gate_2 := cot_ok AND correlation_ok AND commodity_filter_ok AND open_interest_ok

cot_zero_new := (max(net_commercial, 12mo) + min(net_commercial, 12mo)) / 2
cot_ok_long  := net_commercial(now) > cot_zero_new              # printed zero ignored
oi_ok_long   := dOI_pct <= -0.10        # 10-15%+ decline = commercial short covering
oi_ok_short  := dOI_pct >= +0.10        # 10-15%+ rise    = commercial short selling

# --- stage 3: top-down ---
history   := 9..18 months
arrays    := PD arrays on {M, W, D, H4}, all transposed onto the H4 chart
lookbacks := [20, 40, 60]  trading days      # escalate when the shorter one is exhausted
target_zone := arrays  ∩  ipda_lookback_boundaries        # convergence

# --- stage 4: order type is a function of array type ---
order_type(array) :=
    STOP   if array in {breaker, mitigation_block, liquidity_void, fair_value_gap}
    LIMIT  if array in {order_block, rejection_block, beyond_old_high_or_low}

# --- stage 5: management, as fractions of the intended objective D ---
0.00 .. 0.33*D : stop loss untouched
0.25*D         : scale out 20-30% of position ; THEN move stop to break-even (never earlier)
0.50*D         : add back the scaled quarter after a short-term retrace
               : expect a stop run at or just beyond 0.50*D
0.75*D..1.00*D : close the balance ; trail below the most recent H4 short-term low

# --- the swing's internal structure ---
EQ := (range_high + range_low) / 2
stop_run_1 := occurs AFTER price breaches EQ, hunting stops on the far side
stop_run_2 := occurs in the final 1/4 to 1/3 of the move, and is usually the last
projection := fib extensions 1.27 and 1.62 from swing high to swing low,
              accepted where they converge with weekly and monthly PD arrays
```

## Timeframes

Monthly, weekly and daily for framing; **four-hour is the chart everything is transposed onto** and
the executable tier. Trade duration is set by the month's definition of a swing — two weeks or
longer — and ICT states these "only set up around **four to six weeks**" [33:44–34:02].

## Examples

**Example 1 — the worked gold long, Dec 2016 → Feb 2017 (19:17–30:12):**
- **Stage 0:** "December to January there's a strong tendency for the gold market to rally, and then
  continuing from January into February the market generally for the gold market rallies" [19:52–20:11].
  Calendar months read from the **bottom** axis, not the contract-delivery months on top [19:41–19:52].
- **Stage 1:** "the bond markets are trending, the dollar index is trending, the CRB index is **flat**
  going into December, and the stock market is trending" [20:21–20:41] — three of four, one from each group.
- **Stage 2:** commercials rose into the green shaded upper half of their 12-month range in Dec 2016
  ("they have hedged and bought aggressively and **lessened their shorts**"); Williams %R oversold;
  open interest "declined aggressively over a hundred [thousand] contracts"; gold failing to make
  lower lows mid-Dec into Jan **while the dollar index showed a willingness to go higher** — "there's
  the divergence we're looking for"; soybeans, high-grade copper, crude and live cattle all bullish
  [20:49–23:38].
- **Stage 3–4:** the **1142** level is the **monthly bullish order block** — "the last down candle right
  before the up move in **November of 2015**" [23:38–23:53]. Weekly PD array above at **1255–1260**,
  off the 17 Oct 2016 weekly candle; **1200** is the equilibrium of that range [24:11–25:28].
- **Stage 5:** equilibrium breached → the anticipated stop run took the sell stops below 1195–1190,
  price returning to ~1183 and into a **daily** order block at 1175–1180 [27:26–27:50]. Fib extensions
  **1.27 and 1.62** off that swing land on **1260**, "a **confluence** of fibonacci and weekly PD arrays
  and monthly PD arrays" [28:02–28:30]. The **1220–1217** retest after the second stop run is called
  as the swing buy [29:15–29:42].

⚠ **Two transcription/verbal artefacts in this lecture.** (1) At [29:42] the payout is stated as
"1220 all about the 1260s, that's **four thousand dollars per contract for silver**" — the arithmetic
is **gold's** (40 points × $100/point = $4,000 on a 100-oz contract) and every level quoted in the
example is a gold level; read "silver" as a slip. (2) "IPTA data ranges" throughout [12:38–13:12] is
**IPDA**; see [ipda-definition](../23-ipda/ipda-definition.md).

## Common Mistakes

- **Treating the stages as a scorecard.** They are **gates in order**. A later stage is not evaluated
  until the earlier one passes, which is what distinguishes this page from the counting checklists in
  lessons 2 and 7.
- **Reading a failed gate as "no trading".** The stated fallback is always *short-term or day trades
  for now* — a change of discipline, not a stand-down.
- **Skipping the seasonal gate while still calling it the million-dollar setup.** ICT permits dropping
  it, and says so — but names it as the thing that makes the setup what it is.
- **Choosing order type by habit.** Stop-versus-limit is a **function of the array**. Breakers,
  mitigation blocks, voids and FVGs are traded on a **stop**; order blocks, rejection blocks and levels
  beyond old highs/lows on a **limit**.
- **Moving to break-even early.** "Never before" first profit is taken — stated as an absolute.
- **Trailing the stop through the mid-range stop run.** The run at ~50 % of the held range is
  *anticipated*; "don't rush trailing the stop loss."
- **Abandoning the idea after one stop-out.** The procedure explicitly re-enters at a fresh
  monthly/weekly/daily array if the original reasoning still holds.
- **Expecting an indicator.** "You just want to give me a system where I can plug it in my chart and
  it's going to pop up something — **that's not how this is**" [30:42–31:12].

## fair-value-gap

- claim: "3-candle imbalance pattern where … the wicks of the candles before and after fail to overlap" → CONFIRMED (2022) — "it's a three candle formation the first candle's low has to be traded below on the immediate following candle the next candle has to trade with an extended low as well that went below candle number two but does not trade with a high that trades back to candle number one's low" (ep06 [11:42]); "label number one candle the number two candle is always going to be where the gap resides and then the number three candle gives you the lower end the upper end of the fair value gap is going to be the low of candle number one the lower end of the fair value gap is going to be the high of candle number three" (ep06 [16:41]–[17:09]). Candle 2 ("n") owns the gap; boundaries are candle 1's and candle 3's extremes (highs/lows = wicks).
- claim: same geometry, 2016 era → CONFIRMED (Dec 2016) — "we're delineating the low of the previous candle and the high of the count[le] to the right of the down candle that creates that little pocket of space" (`MONTH-04-FAIR-VALUE-GAPS` [04:28]); "See this candle. It's wick and this candle's wick. In between there price was only delivered on the downside. So we have a fair value gap." (`MONTH-04-REINFORCING-LIQUIDITY-CONCEPTS` [07:02]–[07:09]).
- claim: "Wicks are used (not closes)" → ERA-SPLIT inside 2016. Sep 2016 (Month 1): "there's a gap between this candle's low and this candle's body or wick. So I define it by the body. I like to use those." (`MONTH-1-FAIR-VALUATION` [04:41]–[04:51]); and in that lecture the FVG is "the range between this up candle and this up candle … there was no up candle or up movement between the break of that low and the high of this candle" [04:03]–[04:21] — a swing-level, multi-candle one-sided range, not yet a 3-candle rule. Oct–Nov 2016 (Months 2–3) use the term for part of the ORDER-BLOCK candle: "the bullish order block high to open price defines the fair value gap or most probable support" (`MONTH-02-NO-FEAR-LOSING` [02:58]); "We're going to look at the body of the candle, which is the opening on a down candle, up to the high of that candle. Okay, and that's a fair value gap." (`MONTH-02-GROWING-SMALL-ACCOUNTS` [18:31]–[18:37]); "closed in the fair value gap between the high to midway point of the candle" (`MONTH-03-MARKET-MAKER-TRAP-w8lbrvZXUVY` [08:01]). The wick-to-wick 3-candle definition first appears in Dec 2016 (Month 4) and is the only definition in 2022. The page collapses this.
- claim: "Candle n is a strong upward (displacement) candle" / c3 `candle_n_displacement_present == true` → EMBELLISHED. ICT's definitional statements contain no displacement test: "it's only one single candle passing up and the previous candle's High and the next candle's low that area right there is an imbalance" (ep02 [38:14]); "that's a candle with a high one single pass up next candle has a load [low] it doesn't completely overlap all this that's a fair value real simple" (ep03 [13:43]–[14:14]). Displacement is a condition of the TRADE in the 2022 model, located before the FVG search: "that range between here and here that's displacement … start watching to see if it creates a fair value gap in between this low and this candle's high" (ep06 [20:58]–[21:29]); "if there is no fair value gap in here guess what you don't have a trade" (ep06 [21:59]); and price must move away from the FVG before entry: "it has to move away from the fear of a gap" (ep20 [13:52]). Small gaps still count as FVGs: "if you have a fair value gap and a small one right above it anticipate it likely trading up into you" (ep16 [37:50]). The page's "Forgetting displacement … the algorithm leaves 'real' FVGs only when delivery is forceful" is the page's own theory.
- claim: aliases "BISI — buy-side imbalance / sell-side inefficiency", "SIBI" → NOT IN CORPUS. Word-bounded `bisi|sibi`: 0 hits in both corpora. Nearest: "that's a by side imbalance" (ep02 [38:43]), "that little inefficient area" (ep06 [14:42]). Plausibly 2023+ terminology.
- claim: "Year Introduced: 2016" → CONFIRMED — "it's something I introduced back in 2016" (ep02 [30:04]); the term is used from Month 1 (Sep 2016) onward.
- claim: "Year Refined: 2025", Source IDs `ICT-2024-FVG-CLASSIFICATION`, `ICT-2025-CE-PRIMARY-ENTRY`, "immediate vs delayed rebalance" → NOT IN CORPUS (searched `immediate rebalance|delayed rebalance`: 0 both). Outside both corpora' date range; cannot be checked here.
- claim: "where ICT teaches the algorithm tends to return to 'rebalance' the inefficiency" → CONFIRMED — "We're going to expect price to eventually want to trade back up into that little gap area" (`MONTH-04-FAIR-VALUE-GAPS` [06:15]); "it's imbalance rebalance run for liquidity that's it it does not do anything else" (ep12 [72:08]).
- claim: "Tiny FVGs. A 1–2 pip FVG on M5 is noise; filter by ATR." → EMBELLISHED / contradicted by ICT's own examples — "it's only two pips difference, but that creates a what? A gap. So we can be a seller at a more refined price level" (`MONTH-04-FAIR-VALUE-GAPS` [12:37]–[12:42]); 2022 repeatedly trades "a small little fair value gap" (ep18 [33:43]). ICT's selection criterion is the higher-timeframe parent, not size: "The higher time frame imbalances are going to be parent to the subordinate smaller time frames" (ep18 [34:37]).
- claim: "Body-only FVG … that's a different pattern (volume imbalance), not a strict FVG" → partially CONTRADICTED by Sep 2016 "I define it by the body" (above). True for Dec 2016 and 2022.
- claim: "FVGs are ICT's most-cited PD array and the primary entry zone in most setups" → EMBELLISHED; the 2017 PD-array hierarchy lists the order block first: "Then you have bearish order block, fair value gap, and then liquidity void, bearish breaker, and mitigation block." (`MONTH-05-DEFINING-HTF-PD` [25:17]).
- claim: FVG on one TF = liquidity void on a lower TF (Related/liquidity-void link) → CONFIRMED — "in the smaller timeframes, you'll probably end up seeing a liquidity void where the gap would be indicated here on this timeframe" (`MONTH-04-FAIR-VALUE-GAPS` [05:14]).

The one ICT statement against the engine's wick rule is Sep 2016 "I define it by the body" — superseded by his own Dec-2016 and 2022 wick statements; note it, do not act on it.

## nwog

- claim: "NWOG (New Week Opening Gap)" term, Year 2023 → NOT IN CORPUS (`nwog|new week opening gap|weekend gap`: 0 both). Consistent with a 2023 origin; cannot be verified here.
- claim: "price gap between Friday's NY close and Sunday's session open" → partially CONFIRMED by 2022 precursors on index futures: "we had a gap when the session started again last night my local time about six o'clock" (ep34 [00:06], ES); "i'm looking for a gap fill from friday's close so i'm going to go long here 12 micros of the … micro nasdaq futures contract" (ep14 [00:01]). So Friday close → Sunday 18:00 NY session start, on CME index futures. The page frames it as FX ("17:00 NY Friday (FX)… 18:00 NY Sunday (FX broker)") and gives "16:00 NY Friday (futures)" — ICT's own 2022 examples are futures at "six o'clock", and he never names Friday's clock time in either corpus (`friday'?s close` hits are all weekly-range framing, e.g. `MONTH-08-ESSENTIALS-DAYTRADING` [34:07] "the opening price on your monday feed to friday's close that's the range").
- claim: usage as a level → CONFIRMED (2022) — "the opening gap on sunday if you put it in your charts and just plot it across the entire week you'll see that many times there's a lot of valuation around that gap so it'll be treated as a dynamic price level if it's above it look for it to act as support" (ep34 [09:31]); "if it's below it look for it to act as resistance" (ep34 [10:00]); ICT treats it as a zone with an "upper end"/"lower end": "we traded down into the sunday gap i mentioned the lower end would be this time the level to watch" (ep34 [04:57]). Zone boundary = close-to-open, by analogy with the Dec-2016 breakaway gap: "The opening of the gapped up candle and the close of the up candle right before the gap forms." (`MONTH-04-VACUUM-BLOCK` [10:15]); "we're defining it as the high and the low of the gap" [06:19].
- claim: "weekend gaps frequently get filled within the first 1–2 trading days", "tendency to fill within first 1-2 trading days", "NWOG fill to ~75% is common" → EMBELLISHED. ICT: "sometimes the week will just run away from it and won't really come back to it but in weeks where there's consolidation like fomc we have tha[t]" (ep34 [10:25]). 2020: "If we have a gap up, then look for the gap to be filled in and then trade in the direction of the gap opening" (`ICT-2020-OTE-EURUSD-EXAMPLE` [04:42]–[04:54]) — direction rule, no time limit.
- ERA-SPLIT (2017 vs 2020/2022): in 2017 ICT downplayed Sunday gaps — "But in here we have a Sunday gap. Okay, so it's going to be less likely to be that much in terms of an influence. … Now for those without Sunday candles, you wouldn't have this on your chart at all." (`MONTH-09-DAY-TRADE-ROUTINE` [11:31]–[12:01]).
- claim: "NWOGs are larger and more reliable as fill-targets than NDOGs" → NOT IN CORPUS.
- engine divergence: none listed.

## ndog

- claim: "NDOG (New Day Opening Gap)" term, Year 2023 → NOT IN CORPUS (`ndog|new day opening gap|midnight gap|daily open gap`: 0 both).
- claim: "price gap between the prior day's NY-close range and the current day's midnight (00:00 NY) open — the True Day Open" / "prior_close = close at 17:00 NY … tdo = open at 00:00 NY" → CONTRADICTED by every daily-opening-gap passage in the corpus, which places the gap at the 18:00 NY futures session reopen, not midnight: "trading stops at one o'clock in the afternoon on holidays generally and resumes around six o'clock in the afternoon new york time" … "it opened up six o'clock in new york time opened up and slammed down accelerating lower … you have a big gap here it's likely to come back and rebalance that and fill it in and it does" (ep11 [19:04]–[19:59]); "held for the gap opening after reopen at six o'clock" (ep11); "a gap when the session started again last night my local time about six o'clock" (ep34 [00:06]); 2016: "Nonfarm payroll can create a vacuum block or in futures a session open can." (`MONTH-04-VACUUM-BLOCK` [01:03]). Midnight in the corpus is always an OPENING PRICE, never a gap: "new york midnight candle opening price opportunity again it trades up into the new york midnight opening candle trades back into another gap" (ep19 [103:45]) — the gap there is a separate FVG; "midnight starts to cross over to a new day" (ep25); searched `midnight` within 160 chars of `gap` in m22: 5 hits, none describing a midnight gap. On FX (the page's "most relevant" market) there is no close at 17:00 NY in ICT's framing of the day and price trades continuously through 00:00, so "17:00 close vs 00:00 open" is a seven-hour traded range, not a gap.
- claim: "gaps frequently get filled within the same trading day", "~70% of NDOGs partially or fully fill same day; ~30% don't" → EMBELLISHED (no statistic anywhere; the only same-day fill is the single ep11 example).
- claim: "Confusing NDOG with simple FVG. NDOG is a single-bar opening gap (close-to-open)" → CONFIRMED in spirit by the 2016 gap definition ("The opening of the gapped up candle and the close of the up candle right before the gap forms", `MONTH-04-VACUUM-BLOCK` [10:15]).
- claim: "it functions as a vacuum block / opening-gap reference" → CONFIRMED link — "a vacuum block is nothing more than a breakaway gap" (`MONTH-04-VACUUM-BLOCK` [12:48]); "not all gaps fill completely" [12:58].
- engine divergence: none listed. Any engine computing NDOG as 17:00→00:00 on FX would be measuring a range, not a gap; the corpus-supported daily gap is the 18:00 NY futures reopen (close at 17:00 NY, reopen 18:00).

## Cross-page issues (contradictions between pages, inconsistent numbers)

1. Displacement: `fair-value-gap`, `bullish-fvg`, `bearish-fvg` make displacement a formal criterion (c2/c3); `balanced-price-range`, `volume-imbalance`, `fvg-mitigation` and the engine do not. ICT's definitional statements in both eras are purely geometric; displacement belongs to the setup model (ep06 [20:58]–[21:59]). Pages that feed the engine should move displacement out of the FVG criteria into the model pages.
2. Entry depth: `consequent-encroachment` ("CE is the primary entry zone"), `ce-as-primary-entry` (2025) and the worked examples in `bullish-fvg`/`bearish-fvg` enter at CE; ICT 2022 enters at candle 3's extreme (ep06 [17:09]) and explicitly declines the midpoint (ep18 [08:49]). The examples back-date a 2025 rule onto 2016/2022-sourced pages.
3. `fvg-mitigation` makes CE the default mitigation marker while `consequent-encroachment` makes CE the default entry — under both rules the default entry is placed on a zone the other page has just declared "mitigated / no longer a fresh entry zone". Internally inconsistent; neither rule is in the corpus.
4. Sourcing: `fair-value-gap`, `bullish-fvg`, `bearish-fvg` cite the stub `ICT-2016-FVG-INTRO`, while `volume-imbalance` cites the real Dec-2016 packets (`ICT-2016-FVG-REINFORCED`, `ICT-2016-LIQUIDITY-VOIDS`) that actually contain the 3-candle wick definition. The main FVG pages should cite the evidenced packets plus ep06 (2022).
5. Era inside 2016 is unrecorded: Sep 2016 "I define it by the body" and the Month 2–3 use of "fair value gap" for the OB candle's open-to-high are different definitions from the Dec-2016 / 2022 wick-to-wick rule. Any 2016-era study that quotes "the FVG" should say which month.
6. Dates: `fvg-mitigation` and `liquidity-void-vs-fvg` say 2017 while FVG close-in and the FVG/void relationship are both taught in Sep–Dec 2016 (`MONTH-1-FAIR-VALUATION` [22:23]; `MONTH-04-FAIR-VALUE-GAPS` [05:14]). `balanced-price-range` says 2017 for a definition the 2017 corpus does not contain.
7. Futures close time: `nwog` ("16:00 NY Friday (futures)") and `ndog` ("16:00 NY (equities/futures close)") both use the cash-equity close for index futures, while ICT's futures gaps are at the 18:00 NY session reopen (ep11, ep34); the gap's near boundary is the last trade before that reopen, not 16:00.
8. `nwog` and `sunday-open-gap` (outside this cluster) describe the same object twice with different emphases and the same stub source; `sunday-open-gap` also lists "18:00 … sometimes 17:00 NY or 22:00 NY" — the corpus only ever says "six o'clock" (ep11, ep34).
9. Aliases BISI/SIBI (`fair-value-gap`, `bullish-fvg`, `bearish-fvg`) and "volume imbalance" (`volume-imbalance`) are presented as ICT 2016/2022 vocabulary; none appears in either corpus. Mark as 2023+ labels.
10. Size filters ("filter by ATR") appear on `fair-value-gap`, `bullish-fvg`, `volume-imbalance`; ICT trades a 2-pip gap in Dec 2016 and "small little" FVGs throughout 2022. Owner-level choice, not ICT's.

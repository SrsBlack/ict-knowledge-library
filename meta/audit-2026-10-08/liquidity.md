## liquidity-sweep

- claim: "price trading through a liquidity-pool … and then failing to follow through, typically reversing back" → CONFIRMED (2022) — "sweeping is where it just goes above or below a low or high … go up a little bit and then go down and reverse" (ep23 [05:16]–[05:39]); "what i'm expecting as a sweep and reverse back into the range or expansion" (ep23 [05:39]). 2016 usage is the same event, called a stop run: "a low is swept out, that's when you anticipate a turtle soup" (M1-EQUILIBRIUM-VS-DISCOUNT [40:20]–[40:23]).
- claim: "distinguished from a break-of-structure by the lack of close beyond the swept level — sweep wicks; BOS closes" → NOT IN CORPUS. Searched both corpora for "close back", "closes back", "closed back", "back inside" (5 raw / 5 m22 hits, all about retracing into a range or order block, none about a sweep candle's close), "clos(e|es|ed) (above|below)" within lines mentioning old high/low/stops/run/sweep (0 raw). ICT's distinction is behavioural depth/follow-through, not candle close: "running tuesday's high means it runs right over top of it and it just doesn't look back sweeping is where it just goes above or below" (ep23 [05:16]).
- claim: Formal criterion "The candle that takes the level closes back below it" → NOT IN CORPUS (same searches). What ICT does give is a *depth* criterion: "You can expect a 10 to 20 pip sweep below the old low" (M04-LIQUIDITY-POOLS [10:46]); "if it starts moving beyond 25 pips … below the low, it's probably not just a stop run. You're probably looking at a much further decline" ([11:30]–[11:39]); "markets move in intraday price action in grades of 10 … 10 and 20 pit ranges above a high. That's how far they'll reach for a stop" (M1-EQUILIBRIUM-VS-DISCOUNT [51:02]–[51:17]).
- claim: "(high_n - close_n) > 0.6 * range_n [long upper wick]" → EMBELLISHED. The page concedes "ICT teaches it visually". All 10 raw "60 %" hits are fib/accuracy/position-size contexts; 0 in m22. ICT never quantifies the wick.
- claim: level swept is the wick high/low → ERA note (not on page): in 2016-17 ICT repeatedly places the pool at candle **bodies**, not wicks — "we're going to look for a sweep below the bodies of those candles" (M04-LIQUIDITY-POOLS [13:08]); "$109.40 is swept, moving just above the bodies of the candles" (M02-MARKET-MAKER-TRAP-pv2 [09:50]); "Any candle that has wicks above it, we would be noting the bodies of the candle because there would be liquidity resting just above the bodies" (M06-CLASSIC-SWING-TRADING [03:15]).
- claim: "Optional confirmation: a fair-value-gap forms in the opposite direction" → CONFIRMED (2022) — "the market goes up sweeps the highs breaks back down below the highs short-term lows taken out" (ep24 line 68); "stop hunts that lead to market structure shifts" (ep03 [35:33]).
- claim: Year Introduced 2017 → CONTRADICTED — "sweeps out a previous low" (M1-EQUILIBRIUM-VS-PREMIUM [09:10], Sep 2016); "a low is swept out" (M1-EQUILIBRIUM-VS-DISCOUNT [40:20], Sep 2016).
- claim: alias "liquidity grab (SMC)" → NOT IN CORPUS (0/0); correctly labelled SMC.
- engine divergence: engine rule "wick through a prior high/low with close back inside". ICT's words support neither the page's 60 % wick nor the engine's close test as a stated rule; the engine's test is the closer operationalisation of ICT 2022's "sweep and reverse" vs "run … doesn't look back", but ICT's own 2016 quantification is depth (≤ 20 pips typical, > 25 pips = not a stop run), and ICT sometimes defines the level as the body extreme rather than the wick. Divergence to report, not fix.

- claim: "run on liquidity" alias → CONFIRMED — "We are going to sell short in the form of a run on liquidity or a liquidity pool" (M04-LIQUIDITY-POOLS [08:58]); "what you're looking for is a run on liquidity buy stops or sell stops" (ep02 line 95).

## Cross-page issues (contradictions between pages, inconsistent numbers)

1. **Sweep/run hierarchy is inverted relative to ICT.** `liquidity-run` (and `stop-hunt-pattern`'s "run-and-continue") treats a run as the superset containing a sweep, with "run-and-reverse" as one branch. ICT 2022 (ep23 [04:20]–[05:39]) makes them *alternatives*: sweep = shallow penetration then reverse; run = goes through and "doesn't look back". `liquidity-sweep`'s Definition is consistent with ICT; `liquidity-run`'s is not. Any engine/study label "run-and-reverse" is library vocabulary, not ICT's.
2. **Years are inconsistent inside the cluster and wrong against the corpus.** `liquidity-sweep`, `liquidity-run`, `equal-highs`, `equal-lows`, `liquidity-void` say 2017; `external-range-liquidity`, `internal-range-liquidity` say 2022; `relative-equal-highs-lows`, `turtle-soup`, `stop-hunt-pattern` were already corrected to 2016. Every one of these concepts is spoken in Sep–Dec 2016 packets (quotes above). The ERL/IRL "formalized in 2022" claim is the largest dating error (five-year miss).
3. **Tolerance numbers disagree and none are ICT's.** `equal-highs`: "e.g. 2 pips on EURUSD H1"; `equal-lows`: "10+ pips … too loose"; `relative-equal-highs-lows`: "1–4 pips M15–H4, 5–15 pips D/W, a few points, a few cents to a dollar"; `sell-side-liquidity`: "a few pips on FX, a few ticks on indices". ICT gives only "close proximity", "too clean", "relatively equal not exactly". The only ICT numbers in this neighbourhood are the *reach beyond* a level (10–20 pips; > 25 pips = not a stop run), which no page carries.
4. **Strict-vs-relative dichotomy.** `equal-highs`, `equal-lows` and `relative-equal-highs-lows` all define strict EQH/EQL as |Δ| == 0 and REH/REL as 0 < |Δ| ≤ ε. ICT uses the terms interchangeably and calls an exact-tick match "relative equal high" (ep05 [15:31]).
5. **Close-back-inside rule appears on four pages with three different windows** — `liquidity-sweep` "same or following candle" + 60 % wick; `liquidity-run` "with or without close beyond"; `turtle-soup` and `stop-hunt-pattern` "within 1–3 bars" / "within 3 bars". Both corpora: 0 hits for "external BOS", "external structure", "bias change/flip". ICT's ERL/IRL is an entry/exit polarity ("buying inside the range and selling it outside") and a range-relative label.
